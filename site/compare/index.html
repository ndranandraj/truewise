    How to read this. Program counts are of undergraduate programs, as on each college's profile; graduate programs, which the federal rule compares with bachelor's holders, are listed on the profile. A program can be assessed when the Department of Education publishes its graduates' earnings and the state has a high-school benchmark; programs without both are not assessed, and nothing is estimated for them. "Above the high-school line" is the share of assessed programs whose graduates' median earnings were at or above a typical high-school graduate's in that state, the federal earnings-premium test. Earnings are measured four years after graduating where ED publishes them, and one year after where it does not; the two are different career stages, and each college's profile marks the programs that use the one-year figure. Net price is the yearly cost after grants and scholarships. Figures describe past graduates and are never a promise. Source: U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard, release 2026-06-10.
